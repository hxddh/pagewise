# PageWise 9.2 — the record moves to where prose lives, and the reader can write in it

*2026-08-16. Written after 9.0 and 9.1 shipped and the request audit came back clean.*

## 1. Where things stand

9.0 gave the assistant somewhere to write findings; 9.1 sends them back into the
next question and, on the way, turned up that **no per-send context had ever
reached the model** — the hint was assembled every turn and assigned to a field
the SDK does not read.

The audit that followed (`npm run audit:request`) checked the rest of the
request against what the code intends to send. It found nothing else dead: the
system block arrives, all eight tools arrive in the intended order,
`max_tokens` arrives, the hint arrives at 327 characters with its viewing-page
line, and intra-run compaction works — a 6,091-character page read is reduced to
53 characters once two newer reads are in front of it.

That is worth stating plainly, because it decides whether to keep building: the
9.1 carrier bug was an **outlier, not the tip of an iceberg**. The foundation
under 9.0 and 9.1 is real.

**The open question has not moved.** Whether the assistant, given a tool to
write findings, writes *knowledge* or writes *summary* cannot be answered in
this environment — there is no live model. Everything below is designed to be
worth building whichever way that goes, which is a change from how 9.2 was
originally framed.

## 2. Two corrections to the 9.0 design

### 2.1 The record is in the wrong column

The 9.0 design said the record should become "the primary surface", with the
transcript "one tab away". I then put it in the page sidebar, because that is
where marks already were.

Measured, those are not the same place:

| column | width | what it is for |
|---|---|---|
| page sidebar | **160px** | thumbnails, an outline, marks — all short labels |
| assistant column | **360–480px** | prose |

A finding is prose. In 160px its claims wrap to four or five lines, which I
flagged when shipping 9.0 and which no amount of styling fixes: the column is
sized for a thumbnail of a page, and it cannot be widened at a 900px window
without pushing the toolbar back into the overlap 8.1.6 fixed.

"The primary surface, with the transcript one tab away" describes **the
assistant column**. That is where the reader already looks for what the document
says, it is already wide enough, and putting the record there costs nothing —
the transcript keeps the other tab.

Moving findings out also gives the page sidebar back to the page. It returns to
three tabs about *where you are* — Pages, Outline, Marks — and the tab strip
stops being under width pressure at all.

### 2.2 Findings cannot be drawn in the margin

The 9.0 design said findings would render "in the margin, anchored to their
region, in the same visual language marks already use".

**That is not possible and the design was wrong to promise it.** 9.0 established
— from the code, not from preference — that a finding has no region. `addMark`
refuses an empty `rects` because a mark with no rectangle cannot be drawn, and
the assistant reads page text with no coordinates at all. A finding is anchored
to page numbers precisely because inventing a rectangle would be inventing an
anchor.

So margin rendering is cut from 9.2, not deferred. What replaces it is cheaper
and true: a page that carries findings says so, and the record filters to the
current page. The claim stays where it can be read at a readable width.

## 3. The missing half: only one author can write

Marks are the reader's, findings are the assistant's, and the 9.0 design called
them "one record with two authors". They are not, yet. The reader can *strike
out* a finding and can *make a mark*, but there is no way to take something the
assistant just worked out and keep it.

That gap matters more than it looks, because of the unanswered question.

- If the assistant's findings read as knowledge, the record is good on its own
  and this is a convenience.
- **If they read as summary, this is what saves the feature.** A reader who can
  keep the three answers that mattered out of thirty turns has a curated record,
  which is worth more than an automatic one — and it is worth more than nothing,
  which is what a record of mediocre findings is.

It is the hedge against the one risk I cannot measure, which is the strongest
argument for building it now rather than after.

**And the anchor already exists.** `collectReadPages(parts)` computes exactly
which pages a finished answer read — `MessageAssistantFooter` already renders
them as the "Pages read" trail. Keeping an answer needs no new machinery and
invents no anchor: the pages are the ones that answer actually read.

## 4. What 9.2 is

1. **The record moves to the assistant column**, as a tab beside the transcript.
   Prose at prose width.
2. **The page sidebar returns to three tabs.** Findings leave the Marks tab;
   marks stay where the reader made them.
3. **"Keep this" on an answer** writes a finding, with the answer as the claim
   and its own `collectReadPages` as the anchor. Authored by the reader, marked
   as such — the byline already distinguishes the two.
4. **The record filters to the current page**, so turning to page 12 shows what
   is known about page 12. This is what replaces margin rendering.

Cut: margin rendering (§2.2), and a fourth sidebar tab (§2.1).

## 5. Acceptance

Each is checkable here, without a live model:

- A finding renders at prose width — measured, not eyeballed: claim lines wrap
  no more than twice at the assistant column's minimum of 360px, against four to
  five today.
- The page sidebar's tab strip fits with room to spare, at 900px and at 1440px.
- "Keep this" produces a finding whose pages equal that answer's
  `collectReadPages`, and which the next question then carries.
- A kept finding is visibly the reader's, not the assistant's.
- Photographed at 900 / 1200 / 1440, both themes, before it ships.

## 6. What I am still not certain about

- Whether a reader wants a second tab at all, or only ever wants the answer. The
  transcript staying the default tab is the hedge, and it is a real one.
- Whether "keep this" on a whole answer is the right grain, or whether it should
  be a selected sentence. Whole answer first: it is one click, and the claim can
  be edited afterwards since findings are already editable by revision.

## 7. Recommendation

Build 9.2. It corrects two things the 9.0 design got wrong — both of which I
only learned by building it — and it adds the one thing that makes the record
worth having even if the bet in 9.0 does not pay off.
